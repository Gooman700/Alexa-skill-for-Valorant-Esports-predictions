\chapter{Requirements and Analysis}
\label{chap:req_analysis}
This chapter defines the functional and non-functional requirements of the proposed system, establishing a framework for development and a benchmark for final evaluation. These requirements serve as measurable milestones to track progress and ensure the project aligns with its core objectives. To provide a structured implementation roadmap, each requirement is prioritised using the \textbf{MoSCoW} (Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have) method, alongside an estimation of the relative development effort required.

The analysis breaks the overarching problem into distinct, manageable work packages, ranging from data aggregation and model training to voice interface design. Furthermore, this chapter addresses the legal and ethical considerations relevant to public datasets and esports data, ensuring the project adheres to established data protection standards and intellectual property rights.

\section{Project Requirements}
The following project requirements are defined and prioritised using the MoSCoW framework. This categorisation ensures that the core functionality of the Alexa skill is prioritised while identifying secondary features that enhance the user experience. Each requirement is further assigned a difficulty rating on a scale of 1--5, representing the estimated technical complexity and development time. Requirements classified as `Should' or `Could' represent non-essential enhancements that will be implemented iteratively, provided that the primary objectives are met within the project timeline.

\section{Evaluation Strategy}
The evaluation of this project is structured to verify that all functional and non-functional requirements have been satisfied while validating the system's efficacy as a predictive tool. This strategy adopts a multi-dimensional approach that balances algorithmic precision with system performance and user interaction. By addressing these areas, the project ensures that the final deliverable is not only technically sound but also provides a meaningful and reliable experience for the end-user.

\subsection{Machine Learning Model Efficacy}
To evaluate the extent to which the project has satisfied the aim of providing accurate match predictions, the machine learning models will be subjected to rigorous statistical testing. The primary validation method will involve partitioning the VCT dataset into an 80\% training set and a 20\% hold-out testing set, allowing for an assessment of the model's ability to generalise to unseen data. To further mitigate the risk of overfitting and ensure the 60\% accuracy requirement is robust, 10-fold cross-validation will be performed. This technique provides a more reliable estimate of performance by rotating the validation subset across the entire data range. Beyond simple accuracy, the project will utilise a confusion matrix to derive precision, recall, and the F1-score. These metrics are vital for understanding the model's balance between correctly identifying winners and avoiding false positives, which is a more granular measure of success than a single percentage.

\subsection{Alexa Skill Testing}
The non-functional requirements regarding latency and reliability are critical for maintaining a seamless VUI. Verification will be conducted using AWS CloudWatch to monitor the end-to-end execution time of the Lambda function. This tracks the duration from the moment the Alexa Voice Service identifies a user's intent to the final delivery of the prediction. Success in this area is defined by maintaining an average response time of under six seconds, ensuring the system operates safely within Alexa's strict eight-second timeout limit. Furthermore, the robustness of the natural language understanding is verified using the Alexa Utterance Profiler. By testing a library of 100 sample phrases of varying complexity and colloquialisms, the system's ability to map requests to the correct intents is quantified, targeting a minimum successful recognition rate of 80\%.

\subsection{User Experience}
The final layer of evaluation focuses on qualitative satisfaction and the overall fulfillment of the project's original aims. A post-development user survey, conducted under the previously mentioned ethical guidelines, allows a cohort of users to interact with the skill and provide feedback via Likert-scale questionnaires. This assessment covers the perceived naturalness of the conversation and the clarity of the logic provided by the model.

\section{Analysis}
This section will cover the necessary implementation steps needed in order to fulfil the requirements stated in \autoref{tab:functional_req} and \autoref{tab:non_functional_req}. 

\subsection{Data Pre-processing}
The foundation of the predictive engine is the Valorant Champions Tour dataset sourced from Kaggle \cite{ryanluong_dataset}. As established in \autoref{tab:key-dataset-features}, the dataset spans professional match history from 2021 to 2025. A structural analysis of the source files reveals a consistent directory architecture across each annual folder, which facilitates the use of parameterised path references. By utilising variable strings (e.g., transitioning from \texttt{2021\_ids} to \texttt{2025\_ids}), a programmatic pipeline can be established to ingest multi-year data without manual intervention.

To ensure relational integrity across the disparate tables, the project will utilise a Master ID system. Each entity type—including players, teams, and matches—is assigned a unique identifier that serves as a primary key. Before feature derivation can commence, these master tables must be verified for completeness; any missing identifiers would result in reference errors during the join operations.

The data preparation workflow will utilise the \textit{pandas} library \cite{pandas_site} to perform these relational operations. Data from individual years will be read into DataFrame objects and standardised to ensure consistent schema mapping. These DataFrames will be subsequently merged using the \texttt{concat()} function. A critical consideration during this merge is the mitigation of data redundancy; because professional players and teams often persist across multiple seasons, the union of annual tables inevitably introduces duplicate records. These will be identified and purged using the \texttt{drop\_duplicates()} function to ensure each primary key remains unique.

Furthermore, the dataset must be audited for structural anomalies. Data sparsity, often represented as `NaN' (Not a Number) or null values, can significantly degrade the performance of machine learning algorithms \cite{11226584}. To quantify missing data, a diagnostic check using the \texttt{isnull()} and \texttt{sum()} functions will be carried out. Where data is missing, an imputation strategy must be decided—either by removing the incomplete records or by substituting values based on statistical averages—to ensure the final training set is mathematically sound.

\subsection{Feature engineering}
Once the dataset has been ingested and validated for integrity, the project moves into the feature engineering phase. In this phase, raw match statistics must be transformed into a format that captures the ``form" and ``momentum" of the competing teams. To achieve this, a rolling average strategy will be implemented. Rather than providing the model with a static snapshot of a team's history, the system will calculate performance metrics (such as Win Rate, ACS, and KAST) over a sliding window of the previous $n$ matches. This ensures that the model's predictions are based on recent competitive standing rather than outdated historical data, since strategies in Valorant contantly evolve.

The transformation process will also involve the calculation of ``Differential Features." Machine learning models, particularly tree-based ensembles, often benefit from seeing the relative strength between two competitors. Therefore, the implementation will involve calculating the difference between Team A and Team B for key metrics, such as \textit{Differential Average Combat Score} or \textit{Differential Round Win Rate}. This reduces the dimensionality of the input while highlighting the competitive gap between the two teams, which is a primary indicator of match outcomes.

\subsection{Model Development}
With a structured table of rolling features and differentials established, the project will transition to the model engineering phase. This involves defining the architecture for the chosen supervised learning algorithms: Logistic Regression and Gradient Boosted Trees. The implementation will follow a standard ``Train-Test-Tweak" cycle. Initially, a baseline will be established using Logistic Regression to provide a point of comparison for the more complex ensemble model.

The training process will incorporate hyper-parameter tuning to optimise model performance. Parameters such as the number of estimators, maximum depth, and learning rate will be adjusted manually. Following each training iteration, the model will be evaluated against the 20\% hold-out test set using the metrics defined in the evaluation strategy (accuracy, F1-score, and precision). This iterative process will continue until the 60\% accuracy threshold is consistently met or exceeded. Once an optimal model is produced, it will be serialised and exported using the \textit{Joblib} or \textit{Pickle} libraries to allow for efficient loading within the AWS Lambda environment.

\section{Legal and Ethical Considerations}
The development and deployment of a predictive esports skill involves several legal and ethical dimensions, ranging from data ownership to the social implications of AI-driven forecasting.

\subsection{Data Licensing and Intellectual Property}
The primary dataset used for model training is sourced from Kaggle \cite{ryanluong_dataset} and is utilised under its specified academic or open-source licence. Furthermore, match data referenced from VLR.gg \cite{vlr_gg} is used strictly for non-commercial, academic research purposes. The project acknowledges the intellectual property of Riot Games as the developer of Valorant; however, the use of public match statistics for algorithmic analysis is generally considered fair use within an educational context.

\subsection{Data Protection}
As an Alexa-hosted application, the skill adheres to the ``Privacy by Design'' principle. The system does not record or store personal identifiable information. Voice recordings are processed by Amazon's Alexa Voice Service (AVS), and the skill only receives the resulting intent and slot data. No persistent user data is stored on AWS Lambda beyond the duration of the active session, ensuring compliance with the GDPR and the UK Data Protection Act 2018.

\subsection{Gambling Ethics}
A fundamental ethical requirement of this project is to manage user expectations regarding the fallibility of machine learning. It is imperative to communicate that the model's outputs are probabilistic estimates based on historical data, rather than absolute certainties. To mitigate the risk of the skill being misused as a financial advisory tool for gambling, a clear disclaimer will be included on the skill's distribution page. 

Furthermore, the model may exhibit inherent algorithmic bias toward historically dominant teams due to the higher volume of associated training data. To ensure transparency, the skill provides a confidence percentage (e.g., ``65\% chance'') to highlight the uncertainty inherent in the prediction. This encourages users to view the system as an informational tool rather than a definitive source of truth.

\subsection{Content Sensitivity}
While Valorant is rated PEGI 16 due to its depiction of tactical combat and firearm usage, Alexa-enabled devices are frequently positioned in communal household environments accessible to younger children. Ethically, the skill must remain accessible without exposing unintended audiences to mature themes. 

To maintain a neutral and inclusive user experience, the skill's response logic purposefully avoids violent terminology. References to ``kills'' or ``deaths'' are replaced with neutral descriptors such as ``eliminations,'' ``round wins,'' or ``match outcomes.'' By focusing on the strategic and competitive results rather than the violent mechanics of the game, the skill adheres to the safety standards expected of a family-oriented smart speaker ecosystem.

\subsection{Research Ethics and Participant Consent}
The final phase of this project involves a post-development user survey to evaluate the system's efficacy and user experience. Consequently, formal ethics approval has been obtained from the University Research Ethics Committee prior to any data collection. The study is designed to exclude sensitive topics and vulnerable participants. All data gathered is anonymised, and no personal identifiers have been stored, ensuring the research is conducted in accordance with the university's ethical framework for human participation. The approved ethics application for use of the dataset is at \autoref{app:dataset_application}. The approved ethics application for carrying out the user feedback questionnaire is at \autoref{app:questionnaire_application}.
